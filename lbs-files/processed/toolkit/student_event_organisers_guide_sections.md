Design the audience experience
Once you have a clear understanding of your audience, here are some tips for how to design their experience:
Pre-event
•	Keep them engaged between the point of registration and the event
Engage them on social media. Get them talking and posting
•	Send email updates with news about new confirmed speakers or highlight different elements of the event that 	they can look forward to
•	Send them pre-event reminders (one week and one day out) so that they can easily find the info in their inbox
During the event
•	Carefully think about foot traffic and event flow
•	Make registration friendly and easy
•	Provide name badges to facilitate networking if this is important to your audience
•	Everybody loves a goody bag
• Provide plenty of refreshments
•	Allow time for networking and interactions
•	Plan for breaks so that your audience can engage with a clear mind
Post-event
•	Send a follow up email and feedback form immediately after the event
•	Let them know about other upcoming events that they may be interested in, or include save the date for your next 	event

Fundraising and Sponsorship
There are three common ways in which a club can raise funds:
1	Coin Vote - The SA allocates money to clubs based on the number of "coins" that a club collects in the
schoolwide
vote
2	Ticket sales / Subscription - Clubs can sell tickets or collect subscription fees to cover event fees or to support club activities
3	Sponsorship - Connect with organisations who will contribute to the club or event
Sponsorship tips
Have someone in your team dedicated to finding sponsors for an event, or your club for the whole year. A Sponsorship Lead thinks of the longer term goals of your club and builds strategic partnerships with interested companies.
Tips:
1
Start early
- There are many companies willing to sponsor LBS clubs, but they will only sponsor some ‒ so the earlier you get there, 	the better!
2
Set objectives
- Think about your objectives before reaching out to event speakers.  A lead sponsor might ask for the keynote speaker 	slot. This keynote role can often entice the company to commit.
3
Strategise
- Some companies allocate their sponsorship funds at the beginning of the financial year. Others do it last minute to close 	off the year with balanced books. Think about your pitch from your sponsor's perspective: why should they sponsor you? What is their 	return on investment? Can you deliver on it?
4
Shortlist
- Pull together the information from previous years and create a shortlist of companies you would like to contact.
5	Think outside the box - Sponsorship could also mean services or space! If a company cannot offer you any money, they might have a 	great venue you could use for free.
6	What’s the
unique selling point
? Are you hosting an event that could help a company recruit for their next MBA stream programme? 	Are you talking about a hot topic that a potential sponsor is involved in?
7
Work with the SA
- Please discuss your sponsorship strategy with the SA Sponsorship Officer before entering any agreement. You are 	legally bound to the SA and cannot enter a business contract alone.
For every sponsor you secure, you will have to fill a
sponsorship agreement
. You can find the sponsorship agreement template here:
https://clubs.london.edu/LBSSAF/sa-treasury-resources/

## Slide 17

Fundraising and Sponsorship
Sponsorship VAT
If sponsorship comes with no strings attached, the income will be outside the scope of VAT and will not be charged.
If the sponsors get something in return (e.g. free or reduced tickets, participating in the sponsors promotional or advertising activities), then the income is treated as a taxable supply and VAT will be charged.
Sponsorship Contracts
You can only rely on sponsorship money if a contract was signed and legally filed by the SA. Clubs have no authority to enter legal agreements. This protects club officers from being responsible for any liabilities incurred in any agreement.
Please send the final agreement to the SA President, who will arrange for it to be signed. For any further questions, please contact the SA Treasurer.

Communications
Inviting media to your event
In the main, journalists do not report on events they have not attended. Press coverage also tends to focus on newsworthy comment from keynote or other speakers.
If you would like to invite journalists to your event:
·	Consider sending a Media Invitation (see Press invitations below).
·	The invitation should bear: Student Club logo or LBS logo and Student Club logo lockup. For more information 	please contact content@london.edu.
·	Invitations should be individually topped and tailed. Avoid group/impersonal emails.
·	Consider asking your speakers if they have any particular media contacts they would like you to invite on their 	behalf.
·	Aim to send your media invitation two to three weeks in advance of the event. Any earlier and you are unlikely to 	get traction as the journalists will be focussed on news of the day.
·	Copy and paste your Media Invitation into the body of an email rather than sending as an attachment.
·	If interviews are being offered make clear in your invitation – Interviews are available.
·	If your event is being held under Chatham House Rule (journalists can report on what was said but cannot 	attribute what was said to a named speaker), make this clear in your invitation (and also to speakers). Be aware 	that journalists rely on their sources for credibility. It is not impossible, but is significantly less likely, that a 	journalist will report on an event that is held under Chatham House Rule.
·	If your event is a Closed Event, you should not be inviting journalists to this event.
·	Follow up on a Media Invitation a maximum of once, one week out from your event.
·	Media should be offered complimentary event passes.

What is your ticket pricing strategy? If this event has been held before, check ticket prices of previous events and any post-event feedback related to this.

Ticket cost too low > this can negatively impact the perceived value of your event
Research the competition:
